\begin{abstract}
Discovering mechanistic mathematical models directly from raw, noisy physical and biological data remains a core challenge in scientific machine learning. Traditional genetic programming methods suffer from combinatorial explosion and lack physical reasoning, while deep neural networks act as uninterpretable black boxes. In this paper, we introduce \textbf{$E^3$}, a multi-generational, agentic framework that couples Large Language Models (LLMs) as structural mutators with JAX-accelerated Simulation-Based Inference (specifically Approximate Bayesian Computation Sequential Monte Carlo, or ABC-SMC) as parameter estimators. Across 25 distinct synthetic and real-world scientific domains, our current implementation searches continuous-time dynamical equations and records multi-run discovery traces. The strongest recovered models include compact regime shifts, biological compartments, and non-linear physical oscillators, while several domains show little improvement and motivate stricter evaluation. We also outline planned extensions to stochastic jump processes, stochastic differential equations, and reinforcement-learning-aligned mutators, but treat those extensions as future work rather than demonstrated capabilities of the present codebase.
\end{abstract}
