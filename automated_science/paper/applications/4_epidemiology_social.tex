\subsection{Epidemiology and Social Sciences}

Human behavioral systems are highly noisy and network-driven. $E^3$ effectively parsed both epidemiological and macroeconomic datasets.

\paragraph{Epidemiology:} The current epidemiology runs remain continuous deterministic compartmental models. The result table shows many accepted mutations but only a small reduction in the recorded distance, making this a weak domain for the present implementation and a candidate for future stochastic jump-process support.
\paragraph{Social and Economics:} We evaluated synthetic and real-world technology adoption curves. The base exponential model failed to account for market saturation. The engine discovered network-effect saturations, implementing Bass-diffusion-like structures where the adoption rate is proportional to both the unadopted population and the current installed base.
\paragraph{US Real GDP (Real-World):} Macroeconomic growth is notoriously noisy. The current implementation fits deterministic trend and forcing terms to the real GDP series, substantially reducing the recorded distance. A proper stochastic volatility model remains future work.
