\subsection{Physical and Chemical Sciences}

The laws of physics and chemistry provide the most rigorously constrained testbeds for automated discovery. The engine evaluated synthetic datasets generated from fundamental physical relationships.

\paragraph{Astro (Orbital Mechanics):} We tasked the engine with discovering the continuous multi-body orbital decay equations from noisy trajectory data. The LLM successfully introduced inverse-square law approximations and derived angular momentum conservation terms without explicit prompts.
\paragraph{Fluid Dynamics:} Approximating Navier-Stokes turbulence is notoriously difficult. Instead of deriving the full partial differential equations, the engine approximated turbulence regimes using simplified deterministic dampening terms coupled with high-frequency harmonic oscillators.
\paragraph{Belousov-Zhabotinsky (BZ) Reaction:} The BZ reaction is a classic example of non-equilibrium thermodynamics exhibiting a non-linear limit cycle. Starting from a naive linear reaction network, the engine recovered the non-linear limit cycle by correctly deducing the polynomial stoichiometric coefficients required to sustain oscillation.
\paragraph{Chemical Kinetics, Thermodynamics, and Materials:} The engine reconstructed Arrhenius reaction rates by embedding exponential temperature dependencies $e^{-E_a/RT}$. For thermal systems, it successfully deduced Newton's law of cooling and localized thermal dissipation constants. In materials science datasets, the engine discovered power-law stress-strain relations characteristic of non-Newtonian material fatigue.
