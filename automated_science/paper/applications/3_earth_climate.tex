\subsection{Earth and Climate Sciences}

Earth systems exhibit both long-term exponential trends and powerful periodic forcing from seasonal and solar cycles.

\paragraph{Atmospheric $\text{CO}_2$ (Real-World):} We tested the engine against the iconic Mauna Loa Keeling Curve. The engine identified that a simple periodic sine wave was insufficient to capture the variance. It subsequently introduced an exponential anthropogenic acceleration overlaying the seasonal oscillation, decoupling human carbon emissions from the natural biospheric breathing cycle.
\paragraph{Nile River Volume (Real-World):} A notable discovery occurred when analyzing the historical minimum water levels of the Nile River from 622 AD to 1928 AD. The raw data contains a massive, permanent reduction in variance and mean level at the very end of the time series. The engine derived a sigmoidal step-change in the target mean reverting level, explicitly identifying a permanent regime shift centered around the year 1899. While 1899 is a known statistical changepoint in the data, the physical completion of the Aswan Low Dam did not occur until 1902. This discrepancy highlights a critical phenomenon we term \textbf{semantic hallucination}: the LLM successfully discovered the correct mathematical structure (the sigmoidal drop) but occasionally attributes it to historically adjacent or fabricated justifications. This underscores the necessity of human-in-the-loop domain experts to verify the physical or historical semantic outputs of the model.
\paragraph{Solar Sunspots (Real-World):} Sunspot counts exhibit cyclic behavior, but they are highly asymmetric (fast rise, slow decay). The engine recovered the $\sim 11$-year solar cycle frequency ($\omega \approx 0.57$) and introduced a quadratic non-linear term ($+c(S-S_0)^2$) to model the cycle's characteristic asymmetric dynamo shape, drastically reducing the MSE.
\paragraph{Climate and Agriculture:} The engine successfully mapped seasonal crop yield dependencies to multi-variate inputs, generating mathematical structures that thresholded growth based on interacting temperature and rainfall limits.
